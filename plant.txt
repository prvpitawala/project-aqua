## About Myriophyllum Aquaticum

*Myriophyllum aquaticum*, commonly known as **Parrot’s Feather**, is an attractive aquatic plant recognized for its delicate, feather-like foliage. Its finely divided leaves form dense whorls around the stem, giving the plant a distinctive appearance that can add texture and movement to an aquarium or aquatic garden.

The plant is native to South America and is associated with freshwater environments such as streams, ponds, lakes, wetlands, and slow-moving waterways. It can grow both **underwater (submerged)** and **above the water surface (emersed)**.

When grown emersed, the foliage generally becomes more compact and feather-like, which is responsible for the plant's common name, "Parrot’s Feather."

---

## Appearance

Parrot’s Feather has upright stems covered with numerous finely divided leaves. The leaves resemble small green feathers and are arranged around the stem.

### Submerged Growth

When grown underwater, the stems can develop long, soft, finely branched foliage. The plant can create a lush, textured background when several stems are planted together.

### Emerged Growth

When allowed to grow above the water surface, the foliage typically becomes more rigid and densely feathered. Emergent growth can extend above the surface and create an attractive natural appearance in ponds and aquatic gardens.

The plant can produce a dense mass of vegetation under favorable conditions.

## Aquarium Placement

Parrot’s Feather is generally considered a **background plant** because of its potential height and upright growth.

It works particularly well:

* Along the rear of freshwater aquariums
* In planted aquarium layouts
* Around ponds and water gardens
* Near hardscape where taller vegetation is desired
* As a natural background for smaller foreground plants

Planting multiple healthy stems together can create a fuller background and provide visual depth.

## Lighting Requirements

**Requirement: High Light**

Parrot’s Feather performs best under strong aquarium lighting.

Adequate lighting is particularly important for maintaining compact, attractive growth and healthy foliage. Insufficient light can result in weaker or less attractive growth.

For best results:

* Provide strong, consistent aquarium lighting.
* Maintain a regular lighting schedule.
* Avoid sudden changes in lighting intensity.
* Monitor the plant for signs of poor growth or deterioration.

Because lighting needs vary according to aquarium depth, water clarity, plant density, and the type of aquarium light used, the plant should be monitored and lighting adjusted when necessary.

## CO₂ Requirements

**Requirement: Medium CO₂**

Supplemental carbon dioxide can significantly benefit Parrot’s Feather in a planted aquarium.

A stable supply of CO₂ can support:

* Faster growth

CO₂ is particularly useful when the aquarium uses high-intensity lighting. Strong light combined with adequate carbon availability and nutrients can encourage healthier plant growth.

Consistency is important. Large fluctuations in CO₂ availability can contribute to unstable plant growth and algae problems.

## Planting Instructions

When planting Parrot’s Feather in an aquarium, individual stems should generally be planted into the substrate rather than burying the entire plant.

### Basic planting method

1. Separate the plant into individual healthy stems or small groups.
2. Remove damaged or decaying leaves.
3. Plant the lower portion of each stem into the aquarium substrate.
4. Keep the growing portions above the substrate.
5. Leave some space between stems to allow water circulation and light penetration.
6. Provide appropriate lighting and nutrients.
7. Introduce CO₂ if it is part of the aquarium setup.

Avoid crushing or excessively burying the stems, as this can interfere with healthy growth.

Parrot’s Feather benefits from a nutrient-rich planted aquarium environment.

Good circulation helps distribute:

## Pruning and Maintenance

Parrot’s Feather can require regular maintenance when growing strongly.

### Pruning

Trim overgrown stems using clean aquarium scissors. Remove:

* Yellowing leaves
* Damaged foliage
* Dead stems
* Excessively long growth
* Decaying plant material

Healthy trimmed sections may potentially be used as new cuttings if they contain suitable growing portions.

Regular pruning can help maintain the desired height and prevent the plant from shading smaller aquarium plants.

---

## Propagation

Parrot’s Feather can be propagated primarily through **stem cuttings**.

A healthy stem can be cut into sections and replanted under suitable conditions.

### Simple propagation method

1. Select a healthy stem.
2. Cut a suitable section with healthy foliage.
3. Remove excess leaves from the lower portion.
4. Plant the lower portion into the substrate.
5. Provide adequate light, nutrients, and CO₂.
6. Allow new roots and growth to develop.

Under favorable conditions, propagation can produce additional plants relatively quickly.

## Common Problems

### Yellow Leaves

Possible causes include:

* Nutrient deficiencies
* Poor lighting
* Inadequate CO₂
* Poor water quality
* Natural aging of older leaves

### Leggy or Weak Growth

Possible causes include:

* Insufficient light
* Poor CO₂ availability
* Lack of nutrients
* Excessive competition from other plants

### Algae Growth

Algae may appear when there is an imbalance between light, CO₂, nutrients, and plant growth.

Maintaining consistent lighting, adequate CO₂, appropriate fertilization, and regular maintenance can help reduce algae-related problems.

### Melting After Introduction

Aquatic plants sometimes lose existing leaves after being moved between growing conditions, particularly when transitioning between emersed and submerged growth.

Do not immediately assume the entire plant is dead. If healthy portions of the stem remain, new submerged growth may develop after the plant acclimates.
